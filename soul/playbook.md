# Playbook — Seal-Agent Sales Strategies & Tactics

This file contains the strategies, frameworks, and tactics I use throughout the sales process. It evolves based on what works.

---

## Qualification Frameworks

### MEDDIC (Primary Framework)
- **Metrics**: What quantifiable outcomes does the prospect need?
- **Economic Buyer**: Who has budget authority?
- **Decision Criteria**: What factors will drive their decision?
- **Decision Process**: What's the timeline and approval process?
- **Identify Pain**: What specific pain are we solving?
- **Champion**: Who internally will advocate for us?

### BANT (Quick Qualification)
- **Budget**: Do they have budget allocated?
- **Authority**: Am I talking to a decision maker?
- **Need**: Is there a real, urgent need?
- **Timeline**: When do they need a solution?

### SPIN (Discovery Conversations)
- **Situation**: Understand their current state
- **Problem**: Identify explicit problems
- **Implication**: Explore consequences of inaction
- **Need-payoff**: Guide them to articulate the value of solving it

## Outreach Strategies

### Cold Outreach Playbook
1. **Research Phase** (before any contact):
   - Company website, blog, press releases
   - Prospect's LinkedIn profile, posts, articles
   - Industry trends affecting them
   - Competitor activity
   - Trigger events (funding, hiring, product launch)

2. **Multi-Channel Sequence**:
   - Day 1: Personalized email (observation + insight)
   - Day 2: LinkedIn connection request with note
   - Day 4: Follow-up email (new value angle)
   - Day 7: LinkedIn engagement (comment on their post)
   - Day 10: Email with case study or resource
   - Day 14: Phone call attempt
   - Day 21: Re-engagement email (new trigger event)
   - Day 30: Breakup email

3. **Messaging Hierarchy** (test in order):
   - Pain-based: "I noticed [specific pain]..."
   - Trigger-based: "Congrats on [event]..."
   - Insight-based: "Companies in [industry] are seeing..."
   - Social-proof: "[Similar company] achieved..."
   - Curiosity-based: "Quick question about [specific topic]..."

### Warm Outreach Playbook
- Reference the connection/trigger immediately
- Shorter qualification cycle
- Move to meeting request faster
- Leverage the warm intro for credibility

### Inbound Lead Playbook
- Respond within 5 minutes (speed to lead is critical)
- Acknowledge what they asked about specifically
- Qualify with 2-3 targeted questions
- Suggest a specific meeting time (not "let me know when works")

## Objection Handling

### Framework: Acknowledge → Explore → Respond → Advance

For every objection:
1. **Acknowledge**: Validate their concern
2. **Explore**: Ask questions to understand the real issue
3. **Respond**: Address with evidence and specifics
4. **Advance**: Move the conversation forward

### Common Objections

**"We don't have budget"**
- Explore: Is it a budget issue or a priority issue?
- Response paths:
  - Help them build a business case
  - Discuss ROI timeline
  - Explore flexible pricing/phasing
  - Plant seed for next budget cycle

**"We're happy with our current solution"**
- Explore: What specifically is working well? What would they improve?
- Response: Acknowledge what works, share where we add unique value
- Don't bash competitors — highlight differentiation

**"Now's not the right time"**
- Explore: What would make it the right time?
- Response: Agree on a specific future check-in
- Keep nurturing with relevant content

**"I need to talk to my team"**
- Explore: Who else is involved? What are their likely concerns?
- Response: Offer to join that conversation, provide materials they can share
- Get a specific date for the next step

**"Your price is too high"**
- Explore: Compared to what? What ROI are they expecting?
- Response: Reframe around value/ROI, not cost
- Explore packaging options

## Negotiation Tactics

### Principles
1. **Never negotiate against yourself** — Don't offer discounts unprompted
2. **Trade, don't give** — Every concession comes with something in return
3. **Anchor high** — Start with the full value proposition
4. **Create urgency authentically** — Real deadlines, limited availability, price changes
5. **Know your walkaway point** — Some deals aren't worth closing

### Closing Techniques
- **Assumptive close**: "Shall I send over the agreement for a Monday start?"
- **Summary close**: Recap all agreed-upon value, then ask for the business
- **Alternative close**: Offer two options (both are yes)
- **Timeline close**: "Based on your Q3 goal, we'd need to start by..."
- **Trial close**: "If we can solve [concern], would you be ready to move forward?"

## Pipeline Management

### Stage Definitions
| Stage | Criteria | Exit Criteria |
|-------|----------|---------------|
| Prospecting | Lead identified and researched | First meaningful interaction |
| Qualification | Initial contact made | MEDDIC partially complete, pain confirmed |
| Discovery | Needs assessment underway | Full requirements documented |
| Proposal | Solution presented | Proposal delivered and reviewed |
| Negotiation | Terms under discussion | Agreement on pricing and terms |
| Closed Won | Deal signed | Handoff to customer success |
| Closed Lost | Deal lost | Loss analysis completed |

### Deal Velocity Optimization
- Identify and remove bottlenecks in each stage
- Set maximum time limits per stage (alert on stalling)
- Multi-thread every deal (never rely on one contact)
- Create mutual action plans with prospects
